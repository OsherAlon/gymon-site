Used alongside resistance training, creatine tends to add a modest but real edge in lean mass and strength compared with lifting alone. The effect is an average, not a promise, and it varies between people. For creatine for muscle gain, the usual approach is 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate per day, taken consistently while you keep training hard.

## What creatine adds on top of lifting

Creatine does not build muscle by itself. It raises phosphocreatine stores in the muscle, which helps you regenerate ATP faster during repeated high-intensity efforts ([Kidney Stronger summary of the ISSN position stand](https://www.kidneystronger.com/post/benefits-misconceptions-of-creatine-supplementation)). In practice, that may help you squeeze out a little more quality work across your sets. The training itself is still the signal for growth.

That framing matches the research. One recent review of healthy men aged 18 to 30 only included trials that paired creatine with a clearly defined training stimulus ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260410/Want-muscle-from-creatine-Resistance-training-is-the-missing-link.aspx)). The headline calls resistance training the missing link.

A 2026 three-level meta-analysis looks at outcomes from strength through to body composition ([Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15502783.2026.2718316?af=R)). The full text wasn't available to us here, so the numbers below come from other reviews.

## Lean mass and body composition: how big is the effect?

Two meta-analyses give a useful sense of scale:

- **Healthy adults under 50:** A 2024 meta-analysis of 12 randomized trials found that people taking creatine gained about 1.14 kg (2.5 lb) more lean body mass than those who only lifted. They also lost about 0.7 kg (1.5 lb) more fat mass, and body fat percentage dropped by about 0.9 points more ([Mountain Tactical Institute review](https://mtntactical.com/knowledge/research-review-creatine-supplementation-increases-lean-mass-and-reduces-fat-when-combined-with-resistance-training)).
- **Older adults:** A meta-analysis of 16 trials (509 people) found creatine plus resistance training produced a mean lean-mass advantage of 1.32 kg (2.9 lb) over placebo plus training ([Candow et al., Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1912?conv_s=sh%3A1771027200423-0.4486695996019989%2Asi%3A1%2Apv%3A1&conv_v=vi%3A1%2Asc%3A1%2Acs%3A1771027200%2Afs%3A1771027200%2Apv%3A1%2Aexp%3A%7B%7D%2Aseg%3A%7B%7D)).

These are group averages across different programs, so your own result could be smaller or larger.

## Does creatine improve strength?

The ISSN position stand concluded that creatine monohydrate is effective for high-intensity exercise, strength and power, and lean mass ([Kidney Stronger](https://www.kidneystronger.com/post/benefits-misconceptions-of-creatine-supplementation)). The picture gets more nuanced when you look at specific lifts and populations.

A meta-analysis in older adults found a clear improvement in lower-limb strength. It did not find a significant improvement in upper-limb strength, which differs from some earlier reviews ([PMC12752335](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752335)). The authors suggest this might relate to differences in muscle fiber-type distribution between the upper and lower body, but that is a hypothesis, not a proven explanation.

Individual trials are inconsistent too. Of 20 studies in older adults, 10 found benefits for lean mass or strength and 10 found no extra benefit over placebo ([Candow et al., Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1912?conv_s=sh%3A1771027200423-0.4486695996019989%2Asi%3A1%2Apv%3A1&conv_v=vi%3A1%2Asc%3A1%2Acs%3A1771027200%2Afs%3A1771027200%2Apv%3A1%2Aexp%3A%7B%7D%2Aseg%3A%7B%7D)). The authors suggest differences in dose and timing may explain part of that spread.

> **Takeaway:** On average, creatine plus lifting beats lifting alone. A fair share of individual studies still show no clear added benefit.

## How much creatine to take

The standard recommendation is **3–5 g per day** of creatine monohydrate, which a summary of the 2017 ISSN position stand describes as safe and effective ([YourHealthier](https://yourhealthier.com/blogs/blog/best-creatine-supplements)). Monohydrate is the form the position stand's conclusions refer to ([Kidney Stronger](https://www.kidneystronger.com/post/benefits-misconceptions-of-creatine-supplementation)).

### Do you need a loading phase?

The evidence here is mixed, so it's worth being precise:

- A sub-analysis in older adults concluded that a loading phase (20 g or more per day for 5–7 days) mattered for strength gains. It also found that 5 g/day or less seemed enough for upper-body strength, while more than 5 g/day after loading was needed for lower-body strength ([Candow et al., Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1912?conv_s=sh%3A1771027200423-0.4486695996019989%2Asi%3A1%2Apv%3A1&conv_v=vi%3A1%2Asc%3A1%2Acs%3A1771027200%2Afs%3A1771027200%2Apv%3A1%2Aexp%3A%7B%7D%2Aseg%3A%7B%7D)).
- A more recent older-adult meta-analysis included trials using either 5 g/day or a relative dose of 0.1 g/kg/day. Its authors cite a lower-dose protocol without a loading phase as effective ([PMC12752335](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752335)).

Taken together, a daily 3–5 g dose without loading is a reasonable, simple starting point. A loading phase is optional rather than clearly required. If you prefer a body-weight-based dose, 0.1 g/kg works out to about 7 g for a 70 kg (154 lb) person. Pick a time of day you will actually remember, and take it every day, including rest days, so the habit sticks.

## Who is most likely to benefit?

The research in this article covers three groups: healthy adults under 50, young men aged 18 to 30, and older adults. In older adults, creatine plus resistance training increased lean mass and strength, and in three small studies it removed the sarcopenia classification in 11 participants ([Candow et al., Nutrients](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1912?conv_s=sh%3A1771027200423-0.4486695996019989%2Asi%3A1%2Apv%3A1&conv_v=vi%3A1%2Asc%3A1%2Acs%3A1771027200%2Afs%3A1771027200%2Apv%3A1%2Aexp%3A%7B%7D%2Aseg%3A%7B%7D)).

The sources we have don't compare groups like women versus men or beginners versus advanced lifters. Anyone who trains with progressive resistance has a plausible reason to try it, but nobody can tell you in advance whether you will be a strong responder.

### Safety and side effects

The ISSN position stand describes creatine monohydrate as having an excellent safety profile ([Kidney Stronger](https://www.kidneystronger.com/post/benefits-misconceptions-of-creatine-supplementation)). In the older-adult trials that recorded adverse events, the reports were mostly temporary stomach discomfort and mild muscle cramping, with no serious adverse events ([PMC12752335](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12752335)).

If you have a kidney condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or take regular medication, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before starting.

## How to tell if creatine works for you

Because responses vary, test it instead of assuming. A training log makes this straightforward:

1. **Establish a baseline.** Before you start, log 3–4 weeks of your main lifts with load, reps, sets and rest times. Keep the program stable.
2. **Start a steady dose.** Take 3–5 g daily and record the start date.
3. **Change as little as possible.** Keep exercise selection, weekly volume and effort roughly the same, so any shift is easier to attribute.
4. **Track the right numbers.** Look at top-set weight, reps at a given load, and total weekly volume for a few key lifts, such as a squat or leg press and a press. Lower-body lifts are where the evidence for strength gains is most consistent.
5. **Judge over months, not days.** Compare a few months of training with your baseline. If your numbers climb faster or you hold more reps at the same load, creatine may be helping. If nothing changes, that is a legitimate result too, since many trials show the same.

Progressive overload is what turns creatine's small edge into visible progress, and a workout log is what makes that progress easy to see.
