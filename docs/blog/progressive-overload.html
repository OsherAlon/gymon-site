What is progressive overload, without the jargon?

Getting stronger comes down to asking a little more of the body over time. Here is what that means in practice, and how to do it without rushing.

by Gymon Team Sep 28, 2026 5 min read

Progressive overload is the idea that a muscle adapts to the demands placed on it — so to keep progressing, those demands have to grow slowly over time.

More than just adding weight

Adding weight to the bar is the most familiar way to progress, but it is not the only one. A workout can become harder by:

  • One more rep at the same weight
  • One more set for the same exercise
  • A slower, more controlled tempo
  • Better range of motion with the same load

Any of these counts as progress. On weeks when the weight will not move, adding a rep is still a step forward.

Small steps beat big jumps

Big jumps in load tend to break technique. A useful rule of thumb: once every set of an exercise reaches the top of the planned rep range with good form, increase the weight by the smallest step available.

Why a log matters

Progressive overload only works when the last session is known. Writing down weight and reps for every set turns "I think I did 60 last time" into a clear target for today.
