How to estimate your 1RM without maxing out

Ever wondered what you could lift for a single rep? Good news: you don't have to find out the hard way.

In short One heavy set of a few reps can estimate your max, close enough to track your progress.

Drafted with AI assistance from the sources listed below · Not yet reviewed by the editor · Published Oct 7, 2026 · 1 min read

A heavy set already knows your max

Put the weight and the reps into a simple formula, and out comes an estimate. No spotter, no drama, no testing day.

Light, long sets can flatter you

People used to long sets squeeze out more reps at the same weight. In one small study, runners did about twice as many reps as lifters at 70% of their max.

Heavier sets give steadier numbers

The gap between people shrank as the weight went up. So pick a set that feels genuinely heavy, but still clean.

Watch the trend, not the number

One estimate can wobble with a good or a bad day. The same lift, logged the same way each week, tells you if you are getting stronger.

Try it this week

  1. Pick a lift you already do, like a squat or a bench press.
  2. Warm up, then do one heavy set of a few reps, and stop before your form slips.
  3. Pop the weight and reps into the calculator below.
  4. Repeat in a few weeks: same lift, same depth, same rest.

    What it found: Eight weightlifters and eight endurance runners lifted to failure at 70%, 80% and 90% of their leg-press max. Runners did far more reps at 70% (39.9 vs 17.9) and at 80%, but not at 90%.

“Eight weightlifters and eight endurance runners each completed a one repetition maximum test on the leg press and completed repetitions to fatigue at 90, 80 and 70% of their one repetition maximum.
