Deload weeks: stepping back to keep moving forward

A planned lighter week can help hard training stay sustainable. When a deload makes sense, and two simple ways to run one.

by Gymon Team Aug 24, 2026 5 min read

A deload is a planned week of lighter training. The goal is to let accumulated fatigue fade while keeping the habit and the movement patterns.

Signs it may be time

  • Several sessions in a row with performance going down, not up
  • Joints or muscles that stay achy from one session to the next
  • Motivation that has dropped for no clear reason

Some people plan one every 4–8 weeks; others take one only when these signs show up.

Two simple ways to deload

  1. Keep the weights, cut the sets — roughly half the usual number of sets.
  2. Keep the sets, cut the weight — around 60–70% of the usual load.

Either way, the sessions should feel easy.

What comes after

The week after a deload is often a good moment to return to the previous weights — and frequently they feel lighter than before.

A deload is not a requirement, and it is not a fix for pain. Pain that persists is a reason to consult a professional.

General information only, not medical advice. Anyone with a medical condition, an injury or pain should consult a qualified professional before starting or changing a training routine.
